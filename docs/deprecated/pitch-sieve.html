ICLR 2027 · project pitch

Every KV cache is solving
the wrong optimization problem.

Compression methods minimize how well a key is reconstructed. Attention never reads a key — it reads an output. Change the objective and the answer is classical: reverse water-filling, with eviction and quantization as the two corners nobody thought to connect.

Abstract

what the paper says

Serving long-context LLMs is bounded by the KV cache, and every method that shrinks it — quantization or token eviction — is tuned to minimize key reconstruction error. We show this is the wrong distortion measure. Posing KV compression against attention-output error yields a rate-allocation problem whose solution is reverse water-filling: the optimal bit budget for a token is linear in the log of its attention weight, and eviction emerges as the zero-rate case of the same equation. Eviction methods and quantization methods are therefore corner solutions to one program, and both are dominated by the interior — by up to 33× in output error at matched total bits. We realize the interior with a nested code that is provably free to layer, and show that a single refinement pass recovers 81–101% of the oracle allocation.

The problem

why the field is stuck

At 128k+ context the KV cache dominates memory and decode bandwidth. Two families attack it, and both are in trouble:

FamilyAllocatesHow it fails
Eviction
H2O, SnapKV		{0, 16} bitsDrops tokens a future query will need. Missing one critical key produces sharp error spikes in long-output reasoning.
Quantization
KIVI, QJL, TurboQuant		{b, b} bitsAggressive variants lose up to 20 points on AIME25 and ~30% relative on 256k retrieval, and pay 10–68% latency for dequantization. FP8 beats all of them on the Pareto frontier.

These look like unrelated failure modes. They are the same failure: both families fix the allocation a priori, then optimize a proxy objective — how faithfully a key is reproduced — that attention never actually evaluates.

Attention does not consume keys. It consumes \(o=\sum_i a_i v_i\). A key carrying 0.4 of the mass and a key carrying \(10^{-6}\) are not equally worth storing, and no reconstruction metric can tell them apart.

The move

one substitution, and the answer is classical

Swap the distortion measure. Perturb stored keys so that \(\hat s_i = s_i+\delta_i\) with \(\operatorname{Var}(\delta_i)=\tau^2c_{b_i}\), and use \(\partial o/\partial s_i=a_i(v_i-o)\):

$$\mathbb{E}\|\Delta o\|^2\;\approx\;\sum_i a_i^2\|v_i-o\|^2\,\tau^2 c_{b_i}\,,\qquad \sum_i b_i\le B.$$

This is textbook reverse water-filling. Stationarity gives

$$b_i^\star=\log_2\!\big(a_i\|v_i-o\|\big)+c\,,\qquad b_i^\star=0 \ \text{ below the water level.}$$

The zero-rate case is not an analogy. Dropping a set and renormalizing costs exactly \(\sum_{i\in E}a_i^2\|o_R-v_i\|^2\), so eviction's cost constant is \(c_0=1\), derived — sitting on the same axis as every quantized tier. Eviction and quantization become commensurable for the first time, and once they are, the optimum is visibly neither.

a framing we discarded This project began with an intuitive heuristic: spend precision where the selection boundary is uncertain. The theorem says the opposite. Boundary tokens carry small \(a_i\) by construction, so \(b_i^\star\) is lowest at the cut and highest deep in the head — as far from any boundary as a token can be. The optimal allocation is monotone in attention mass, not peaked at a threshold. We report this because the discarded heuristic is the one most people reach for first, and because it is exactly the kind of plausible intuition a correct objective function overturns.

Design space

five ways to occupy the interior

A per-token allocation needs a code readable to variable depth. TurboQuant's random rotation makes rotated coordinates asymptotically Gaussian, and Gaussian sources under squared error are successively refinable (Equitz–Cover) — so a nested code meets the rate-distortion bound at every layer. The rotation that makes the quantizer near-optimal is the rotation that makes it layerable at no cost. Given that, the design axis is how the ladder gets chosen:

D1 is the pick, with D2 as the ablation that isolates what per-head adaptation is worth, and D5 as the fallback if kernel work stalls. Storage is fixed at 8-bit keys plus FP8 values — precisely the k8v4 budget, independently measured at BF16 parity — so memory is unchanged and the entire win is bandwidth. Layer-major storage keeps every pass a contiguous strided read plus a gather, which is what stops per-token mixed precision from dying on ragged access.

Two softer benefits matter as much in review. The worst case is bounded: when a head is diffuse and the ladder collapses, the method degrades continuously toward uniform precision rather than breaking — it stops winning before it starts losing. And it subsumes rather than competes: at low budget and high sharpness the optimum does evict 90%+ of tokens, so sparse attention is recovered as a limiting case with a stated validity range, converting a rival literature into a corollary.

Novel claims

each one falsifiable

  1. The objective is wrong. KV compression should minimize attention-output distortion, not key reconstruction distortion. Everything else follows from this one substitution. theory
  2. Optimal precision is logarithmic in attention mass: \(b_i^\star=\log_2(a_i\|v_i-o\|)+c\). The measured dynamic range of \(a_i\) is 13–21 bits, so uniform bit-width is not a mild approximation to the optimum. confirmed
  3. Eviction is quantization at zero rate. \(c_0=1\) is derived, not posited; H2O and KIVI are corner solutions to one program, and each loses badly in the other's regime. confirmed
  4. The optimal ladder is \(\tau/\ln2\) bits wide, where \(\tau\) is the per-head logit spread — making the ladder a measured property of a model rather than a hyperparameter. confirmed
  5. Unbiased quantizers make bad indices. Selection depends on the score vector only through its ordering, so monotone bias is free and variance is not. QJL's variance penalty pushes its 1-bit tier above the cost of eviction and knocks its 2-bit tier off the convex envelope: with QJL, those tiers are never optimal at any budget. This independently explains why practitioners deleted QJL from the deployed implementation. confirmed
  6. Layering is free. Random rotation places rotated keys in the successively-refinable Gaussian regime, so a nested code costs no rate over a monolithic one — the enabling result for per-token allocation. to test on real keys
  7. Spend the byte budget on dimensions, not bits. Bits buy exponential error reduction, dimensions only \(1/\sqrt{d'}\); full-dimension low-precision dominates low-dimension high-precision at every budget. A direct prediction against learned low-rank indexers. to test
  8. One cascade iteration suffices to resolve the circularity that \(b_i^\star\) depends on \(a_i\), which depends on \(b_i\). confirmed

A framework that only reorganizes known results gets scored as a survey. This one made four predictions before we ran anything, and all four held.

What happens next

and what would stop it

The project now rests on one number. Both the noise and the signal scale with \(\tau\), so it leaves per-bit SNR fixed while setting the width of the optimal allocation — which makes it the single parameter deciding whether allocation pays at all. The break-even is sharp:

\(\tau\)ladder widthgainverdict
< 1.0< 0.9 b1.0–1.4×theorem correct, method inert — stop
1.0–1.50.9–1.4 b4–24×narrow the paper to retrieval heads
> 1.5> 1.4 b14–70×full paper

Reproducing the top-1 attention weights of 0.1–0.3 typical of retrieval heads requires \(\tau\approx2.5\text{–}3\), placing them in the top band — but that is an inference from a Gaussian model, not a measurement. Measuring per-head \(\tau\) on three production models takes one day of forward-pass hooks, gates every downstream decision, and is a publishable characterization in its own right.

After that: order-preservation on real keys (2 days), nested-coding overhead (1 week), GQA union cost (1 week), then the kernel and the iso-budget ablation on hardware (4 weeks). Roughly seven weeks to a submission-grade result, with a kill gate at every stage.

Simulation: \(L{=}32{,}768\), \(d{=}128\), 6 logit realizations × 24 noise draws, TurboQuant's published distortion constants, idealized iid \(q,k\) coordinate model. No result has yet been validated against a real KV cache. Deployment claims — the omission of QJL, the meaning of norm correction, accuracy and throughput against FP8 and BF16 — come from the vLLM project's May 2026 study and the merged attention-backend implementation.
