§ 1 Verdict

The self-score of 8.5 is computed against the wrong baseline

Bottom line

RDKV published your framing — output distortion as the objective, reverse water-filling as the solution, eviction recovered as the zero-rate endpoint. Their Theorem 3.3 even uses the words phase transition for the water level. Proposal contributions #1 and #2, and pitch claims C1 and C2, are prior art and must be deleted as contributions.

What RDKV does not have, and cannot get from its experimental design, is an order parameter: they have the transition, you have the variable that says which side of it a head is on. That is a real paper. It is not the paper either document currently claims to be.

§ 3 Blocking

Three things in the draft that will get you killed

Blocking · aτ/ln2 is a tautology, and you call it your strongest evidence

ladder_bits = std_i(log₂(aᵢ·wᵢ)), and log₂ aᵢ = sᵢ/ln2 − log₂Z, so std(log₂ a) = τ/ln2 exactly. The value term you separately measured to be decorative (≤0.035 bits) is the only thing standing between prediction and identity. Your 1.5% worst-case error is the size of a term you have already told the reader is negligible. The right panel of Fig 5 plots x against x. This has survived from v5 through v8, and one sharp reviewer ends the paper’s credibility with it in two sentences.

FixDemote to an appendix consistency check. Promote the number you buried in §3 instead: predicted/measured gain now runs 0.80–1.35. That is a first-principles cost model predicting a measured output-error ratio within ±35% across 22 configurations — a real forward prediction of the same theory, and your best number.
Blocking · bContribution #6 is false about the field

“The field’s eviction baselines are oracles.” H2O, SnapKV, AdaKV, StreamingLLM and RDKV all score on observed attention. The oracle corner was your bug, not theirs, and a reviewer will say so in one line. The defensible version is more interesting anyway, and connects straight to RDKV’s own stated limitation:

Replacement claimScores computed from the full uncompressed prefill attention matrix — RDKV, CAOTE, CriticalKV — are available once, at prefill, and unavailable during decode. Methods that freeze the allocation after prefill never pay for this; methods that re-budget during decode do. We measure the price: 1.11–1.41× in error, concentrated on diffuse heads.
Blocking · cThe two documents contradict each other, and the pitch is stale

The pitch still ships the refuted budget mis-specification as claim C6 “new, testable”; still lists “the eviction corner is an oracle” under What is not yet settled; still says “at 128k only llama3.3-70B holds NARROW; everything else is STOP.” Its header says 22 configs / 39,424 heads while its abstract says 20 / 33,920. C9 quotes the 70B at 79%/59% where both tables say 82.1/61.4 (oracle) or 92.6/75.4 (practical). Fig 5 in the proposal is captioned as pending regeneration.

And cut the confession“We replace our results table entirely, for the second time” plus five named defects invites the reviewer to assume there is a sixth. Rigor is proven by the validity gates you now run, not by the length of the archaeology. Keep the gates in the main text; move the bisects to an appendix.
RepositionStop making a fragile statistic the y-axis

Your §6 already concedes that “the band fraction is a fragile statistic; the error ratios are the robust ones.” Then replot Fig 5-left with median routed gain on y, band fraction demoted to a secondary panel. That deletes the threshold-fragility objection and the unstable “+10.6 to +28.9 points” framing in one move. Also report the correlation partialled on model identity — 22 configs nested in 6 models are not 22 independent points, and a reviewer will say so.

03

Ship the thing their Limitations section says they can’t

RDKV: “compresses the KV cache once after prefill and does not re-evaluate during decoding… Future work: streaming re-budgeting.” Your cascade plus lagged score is that — and you now have the motivation nobody else has: τ rises monotonically with L, so an allocation calibrated at prefill is provably mis-specified by the end of a long generation. A mechanism-backed reason to re-budget, not a hand-wave.

weeks · your strongest route from characterization to method

04

K*-derived per-head budget allocation

AdaKV allocates budget across heads by attention concentration. Your K* result says concentration is the wrong signal — the 70B has the smallest n₉₅ in the study and the largest tail dependence, a 36× spread that n₉₅ does not order. K* is computable in one cumulative pass over ranked tokens. Replacing AdaKV’s budget rule with a K*-derived one and measuring on LongBench is a small, self-contained win against a named baseline, orthogonal to whether the interior pays.

~1 week · a second, independent contribution

05

Scope the K-channel axis, or lose points for missing it

RDKV does per-token V and per-channel K (ThinK-style rank-one weights, their Prop 3.2). You do per-token K only. Either extend the phase analysis to the channel axis — does the channel-side ladder have its own dead tiers? — or state in §1 that you study the token axis and that the channel axis is orthogonal and composable. Silence reads as an oversight.

a sentence, or a week · either is fine; silence is not

§ 5 Rewrite

Motivation and contributions, redrafted

The current thesis paragraph — “every method minimizes reconstruction distortion; posed against output distortion it becomes rate allocation with eviction as the zero-rate case” — is, sentence for sentence, RDKV’s abstract. Concede it in the first paragraph and pivot. Replacement copy:

Draft — opening

Three recent systems — RDKV, HqeKV and ARKV — independently converge on the same formulation: KV cache compression is one rate-allocation problem in which eviction is the zero-rate end of a single curve. We take that formulation as established. What none of them establishes is when the interior of the curve is worth having. RDKV’s own ablation answers this accidentally and unfavourably: on LLaMA-3.1-8B a three-tier scheme lands within 0.5 points of its full mixed-precision allocator, and its advantage over eviction baselines on RULER collapses from 12.3 points at 32k to 4.1 at 128k. Neither observation is explained there, and neither can be, because the framework contains no variable that distinguishes a head where allocation pays from one where it degenerates into eviction.

We derive that variable from the same cost comparison that generates the allocation. A tier at b bits is worth buying only while τ²c_b < c₀ = 1; the fraction of heads whose low tiers have gone extinct predicts the measured advantage at ρ = −0.96 across 22 model×context configurations, 39,424 heads, six architectures and five octaves of context. It contains no L — and that matters, because context length turns out to be the steepest axis in the study. τ rises monotonically with L in all six models, extinguishing rungs from the bottom, and the interior’s advantage decays 3.5–10.3 points per context doubling. The joint-allocation literature is being validated at 4k–32k, and the mechanism it depends on is fading at the lengths that motivate it.

That framing does four things at once: it cites the scoop, makes it the premise, uses the scooper’s own numbers as your motivating evidence, and states a claim no one else can make. It is also a more interesting paper than “our method wins.”

Ten contributions become four

Reviewers read four. Everything below the line becomes a Findings and negative results subsection.

  1. A regime variable, derived not fitted, that predicts when joint allocation beats its own corners. Dead-tier fraction, ρ = −0.96 over 22 configurations; invariant to a 10–29 point shift in its target when the eviction corner is redefined. Your current #3, unchanged — it is the paper.
  2. Context length is a phase variable, acting through τ. One model traverses the diagram on L alone; per-model verdicts are ill-posed, which invalidates how this literature — ours included — reports results. The decay is visible and unexplained in the published state of the art’s own tables.
  3. A cost model that predicts measured output-error gain to within 0.80–1.35×, and a router that reads each head’s phase from one calibration pass. The calibration extrapolates across deployment context because the variable is L-free. Replaces the circular τ/ln2 claim with the real prediction, and folds §5’s router in.
  4. Two measured facts that redirect system design. Scoring is solved and allocation is not — the oracle beats deployable H2O by only 1.11–1.41×, concentrated on heads the router sends to the interior anyway. And tail dependence is architectural: K*/n₉₅ spans 36× and is not ordered by support, so no fixed-fraction or fixed-κ budget can serve a model set.
Demote to appendix or findings section The value term, the QJL dead zone, the φ retraction, the Gaussian-simulation result, the defect record, the 1-bit tier. Two demotions worth naming explicitly: #5 (τ/ln2 to 1.4%) goes to an appendix consistency check for the reason in §3a, and #6 (oracle baselines) gets rewritten per §3b and folded into C4. These are good results; they are not headline slots, and ten contributions reads as a lab notebook.
§ 6 Binding
constraint

The one experiment you still owe

Your own “ways this dies” list has it right at #3, and it is the binding constraint. With RDKV on the table showing LongBench, RULER and InfiniteBench plus a 4.5× decode speedup, a pure output-error paper draws why should I believe this matters.

Cheapest decisive testRouter-on vs router-off, two models, matched budget

Pick your highest dead-tier and lowest dead-tier model. Run RDKV-style allocation with the router enabled and disabled at matched budget on RULER. Show the router recovers accuracy exactly where the diagram predicts the allocator degenerates. One model pair, a few days.

What it buysIt converts the paper from characterization to characterization-plus-actionable — the difference between a reviewer saying “interesting map” and “I would run this.”

Net: with the RDKV repositioning, the four-contribution rewrite, the τ/ln2 demotion, the RDKV-as-experiment figure and one end-task pair, you land at 50–60% — roughly where your v8 self-score sits, but for reasons that survive contact with a reviewer who has read the prior work.
