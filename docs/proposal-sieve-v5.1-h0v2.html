A Phase Diagram for KV Cache Compression

Uniform quantization, per-token rate allocation, and token eviction are each optimal in a different regime. The boundary variable is derivable from the same cost comparison that generates the theory, it contains no context length, and context length is what moves models across it.

Thesis. Every KV compression method minimizes a reconstruction distortion. Attention does not consume keys; it consumes an output \(o=\sum_i a_iv_i\). Posed against output distortion, KV compression becomes a rate-allocation problem whose solution is reverse water-filling, with eviction recovered as the zero-rate case at derived cost \(c_0=1\). The three families are therefore one program — and the program tells you which of them to use.

What the completed H0 changed. The paper is no longer "our method wins", and it is no longer even "each method wins somewhere". It is: the regime is a function of (architecture, context length), the boundary variable falls out of the theory rather than being fitted, and one model traverses all three regimes as context grows. A per-model verdict — which every prior version of this document reported — is an ill-posed object. Along the way the measurement exposed a mis-specification in how the field defines eviction budgets that plausibly accounts for the corner's apparent dominance at 128k.

The architecture ranking is not stable across context. llama3.3-70B is the best model in the study at 8k–64k and the second worst at 128k. "Which phase is this architecture in" is only well-posed at a stated context length — which is the thesis, now demonstrated at its own expense.

The decay is −10.0 points of band per context doubling, monotone across all five octaves. The same slope measured on every model in the set: llama3.3-70B −10.3, qwen1.5-MoE −9.0, qwen3-8B −6.9, mistral-7b −5.2, qwen3-30B −3.5. Context is the steepest axis in the study — steeper than architecture — and the practical corner does not change that, it only moves the intercept.

Every link in the chain is measured. \(\tau\) rises monotonically with \(L\) in all six models (within-model rank correlation 1.00). Rising \(\tau\) pushes \(\tau^2c_b\) past \(c_0=1\) for progressively higher rungs, so the dead-tier fraction climbs, the ladder is eaten from the bottom, and the optimum degenerates toward the two-tier solution that is eviction. Gain over uniform actually grows with context (23×→33×) — the interior is not getting worse in absolute terms; the eviction corner is catching up.

The second sweep separates two distinct causes of the same symptom. qwen3-30B moves barely at all (10.3% → 4.1% over 16× context) and carries 60–73% dead tiers at every length: it is pinned in the eviction corner by sink-dominated attention (median top-1 ≈ 0.5), architecturally, not because the context is long. llama3.1-8B's steep traversal is the opposite. Same corner, different mechanisms, distinguishable on the same axis.

The mis-specification hypothesis — now tested, and refuted v7 proposed that the eviction corner might be winning at long context only because a fixed-fraction budget (\(B\!\cdot\!L/b_{\max}\) tokens, linear in \(L\)) hands it more slack than any head can use. We built the test and the answer is no. Define \(K^\*\) as the smallest keep-count that lands within 10% of the full-budget corner's error. Across six models and every context from 8k to 128k, \(K^\*\) is 100% of the budget — the corner genuinely needs every token it is given. There is no slack to reclaim, and the absolute-support budget we proposed (\(\kappa\,n_{95}\), \(\kappa{=}4\)) is not a cheaper corner but a broken one, costing 1.9–7.4× the error for its bit savings.

The diagnostic did not come back empty, though — it came back with a different finding. \(K^\*/n_{95}\) spans 36× across the set (6.5 for mistral-7b to 233 for llama3.3-70B), and it is not ordered by support: llama3.3-70B has the smallest \(n_{95}\) in the study (49 tokens at 32k) and the largest tail dependence, needing ~10,000 tokens to stay within 10%. Two models can concentrate 95% of attention mass identically and still need order-of-magnitude different keep-counts. This is why no global \(\kappa\) can work, and it is the number that should drive per-head budget design.

3. What survived, what broke

Confirmed, and stronger than in v6 C4 — the linearized cost model forward-predicts the measured gain. Predicted/measured output-error gain at 3 bits runs 0.79–1.34 on per-configuration medians across all 24 configurations, against the 0.93–4.89 reported in v6 — the 4.9× overstatement was an artifact of the corrupted cache, not a property of the theory. Per-head scatter is wider (p10–p90 ≈ 0.39–2.58×), so the claim is stated at the aggregation level where it holds. This is the theory’s only genuine forward prediction, and it is now the headline evidence: a first-principles cost model landing within −21%/+34% of an exactly-recomputed error ratio — a quantity that was free to be wrong by 5×, and previously was. It is also corner-independent, being a statement about the allocator’s own cost function rather than about any baseline.

The value term is decorative. Dropping \(\|v_i-o\|\) moves the ladder by at most 0.035 bits anywhere in the dataset. Allocation reduces to \(b_i^\star=\log_2 a_i+c\), reads only attention weights, and never touches the value cache. Built as an ablation against our own design; it simplified the method.

Retired: “ladder width = τ/ln2, confirmed to 1.5%”. This was billed as the strongest single piece of evidence in the paper and it is an algebraic identity. Since \(\log_2 a_i = s_i/\ln2 - \log_2 Z\) and \(Z\) is constant in \(i\), \(\operatorname{std}_i(\log_2 a_i) = \tau/\ln2\) exactly — verified at \(8\times10^{-16}\) relative error per head across all 24 configurations and pinned by tests/test_units.py::test_ladder_identity. The only empirical content is the value term, which the line above measures at ≤ 0.035 bits; so “agreement to 1.5%” was measuring the size of a quantity we simultaneously describe as decorative, and Fig 5-right plotted \(x\) against \(x\). Demoted to an appendix consistency check; the forward prediction above takes its place. Nothing in the data changes — τ/ln2 remains the right way to compute ladder width and the routing signal built on it (§5) is unaffected.

The quantizer's temperature distortion is a universal constant. \(\alpha=0.941\)–\(0.944\) at 2 bits across all 24 configurations. One scalar, model- and context-independent, correctable offline.

The phase axis survives a change of baseline — the strongest robustness evidence in the paper. The dead-tier fraction was fitted against a band fraction measured with an oracle eviction corner. Rebuilding the corner as a real evictor moves every band fraction by 10–29 points, and the correlation is unmoved: \(\rho=-0.978\) against the oracle band, \(\rho=-0.945\) against the practical one. A regime variable that survives a 29-point shift in its own target is not an artifact of how the baseline was specified. \(\tau\) tracks it at \(-0.78\), and \(\varphi=n_{95}/L\) remains null-to-wrong-signed (\(+0.04\)).

And it survives the statistical objection too. 24 configurations nested in six models are not 24 independent points. Partialling model identity out of both ranks moves \(\rho\) the right way — to \(-0.967\) on routed gain (\(-0.940\) on the band fraction) — and the relationship is perfectly monotone, \(\rho=-1.00\), within every one of the six models. The dead-tier fraction is therefore not riding on between-architecture variance: it orders context lengths correctly inside each architecture as well as across the set. One honest refinement this exposes: on the within-model view ladder width does just as well (partial \(-0.973\)), and it is only in the pooled, cross-architecture view that dead-2 pulls decisively ahead (\(-0.94\) vs \(-0.81\)). That is the expected division of labour and it matches §5: dead-2 is the \(L\)-free variable that transfers between architectures and context lengths, while ladder width is the cheap per-head signal the router actually reads inside a given model.
Broken — and these belong in the paper, not the rebuttal H2 fails at the stated threshold. Spearman \(\rho\) between 2-bit scores and true logits over the top 1% is 0.52–0.77 against a 0.9 kill criterion. M3's order-preservation claim does not hold at 2 bits. The survivable reading is that ranking within the top 1% is a much harder test than separating head from tail — all the cascade's first pass needs — but the claim weakens to "the coarse pass identifies the head region, not the order within it", and the base layer moves to 3 bits.

The 1-bit tier is dead essentially everywhere. 95–100% of heads in every configuration. It should be removed from the offered set rather than allocated and then never chosen.

The band narrows with context — but far less than v7 reported, and the reason we gave was wrong. v7 said the 128k verdicts were STOP and blamed a mis-specified budget. Both halves have since been tested. The budget hypothesis is refuted: \(K^\*\) is 100% of the fractional budget at every model and every context, so there is no slack to reclaim. The artifact was the corner's scoring rule, not its size — it was an oracle. Against a real evictor llama3.1-8B holds 28.8% at 128k, NARROW rather than STOP, and no configuration in the study is STOP. The decay is real and steep (−3.5 to −10.3 points per context doubling) but it no longer crosses the kill line inside the measured range.

The 128k row is complete, and it is the tightest part of the study. All three models that reach 128k natively now have a point there: llama3.1-8B 28.8%, llama3.3-70B 25.0%, qwen3-30B 18.6% — NARROW, all of them, none STOP. The margin is thinnest for qwen3-30B at 3.6 points above the kill line. A second, independently-configured run of llama3.3-70B at 128k (a corner set without recency, structurally immune to the §3 defect) reproduces its band to 0.21 points — 24.96% against 25.18%.

Five measurement defects each produced a plausible, wrong answer. A synthetic haystack (8 sentences tiled ~970×) made every head look diffuse; a top-left-aligned causal mask under chunked prefill corrupted the KV cache beyond the first 4,096 tokens of every prompt, which is what produced v6's entire results table; the validity gate we built to catch the first was keyed on a bulk moment that a single needle cannot move, so it vetoed five good models; the eviction corner ranked by an oracle score no deployable system has, deflating every band fraction by 10–29 points; and adding a recency evictor silently corrupted the corrected campaign, because recency is the only evictor that scores with no history, so on the first decode step the min-over-corners collapsed onto the weakest one — inflating the band by 29 points on average and turning the band-vs-context curve non-monotone. That last one is the sharpest methodological lesson in the paper: a baseline defined as a minimum over several methods silently becomes the weakest of them wherever the others are undefined. It was caught because an earlier campaign that happened to lack the recency corner disagreed by 29 points and agreed with the filtered data to 0.5. Each is documented with the bisect that isolated it and a regression test. We report this because a reviewer's first question about an attention study at this scale should be "how do you know your probe is right", and the answer is "we broke it three times and here is how we caught each".

4. The allocation theorem

With \(\hat s_i=s_i+\delta_i\), \(\operatorname{Var}(\delta_i)=\tau^2c_b\), and \(\partial o/\partial s_i=a_i(v_i-o)\), first-order output error is \(\sum_i a_i^2w_i^2\tau^2c_{b_i}\). Minimizing under \(\sum_ib_i\le B\) gives, above the water level,

$$b_i^\star=\log_2 a_i+c \qquad\text{(the } \|v_i-o\| \text{ factor is measured to be negligible, §3)}$$

and \(b_i^\star=0\) otherwise. Eviction's cost constant is derived, not chosen: dropping a set \(E\) and renormalizing costs exactly \(\sum_{i\in E}a_i^2\|o_R-v_i\|^2\), so \(c_0=1\) in absolute units and eviction sits on the same axis as every quantized tier. The comparison \(\tau^2c_b\) vs \(1\) is what generates the tier-extinction boundary of §1.2.

5. Architecture — the router is the method

v5 treated per-head routing as a safety valve. The completed measurement promotes it to the central component, and adds a requirement: the phase depends on the deployment context length, so the router must be indexed by (head, \(L\)) — but because the boundary variable is \(L\)-free, one calibration extrapolates instead of needing a sweep per deployment.

OFFLINE CALIBRATION (one forward pass; the H0 pipeline as shipped)

  the test is derived, not fitted:  buy tier b only while tau^2 * c_b < c0 = 1

  ladder width tau/ln2 < ~2 b     -> UNIFORM bits, allocation disabled
  2-bit tier live, dead frac <45% -> SIEVE ladder, tiers from the convex envelope
  dead-2 fraction  >= ~45%        -> evict-DEFAULT, but NOT wholesale: against a
                                     real evictor these models still hold 24-27%
                                     of heads in band, so the per-head override
                                     below decides, not the model-level phase
  1-bit tier                      -> never offered (dead in 95-100% of heads)

PER-HEAD OVERRIDE (the model-level phase is only a prior)
  route to the ladder when the HEAD is diffuse, not when the model is:
  in-band heads carry 8-18x the n95 of out-of-band heads in the same model.

  tau rises monotonically with L, so a head can be re-phased for a new deployment

STORAGE  nested code, 8 b K + FP8 V = k8v4 budget, 2.0x vs BF16, layer-major

Measured consequence at 32k against a real evictor: the router sends 23.6–75.4% of heads to the ladder across all six models, for routed gains of 1.53–3.11×. No model is now declined wholesale — the two Qwen3 models, previously handed ~94% of their heads to a corner, keep a quarter of them in the interior.

6. Contributions

  1. The objective is wrong. KV compression should minimize attention-output distortion, not key reconstruction distortion. Everything follows from this substitution.
  2. Eviction is quantization at zero rate; \(c_0=1\) is derived, not posited, putting two literatures on one axis for the first time.
  3. A phase diagram whose boundary variable is derived rather than fitted — and survives a change of baseline. The dead-tier fraction — heads with \(\tau^2c_b>c_0\) — predicts the productive band at \(\rho=-0.95\) across 24 configurations, six architectures and five octaves of context. It contains no \(L\). Critically, it was established against a band measured with an oracle eviction corner; replacing that corner with a real evictor moves every band fraction by 6–29 points and the correlation is unmoved (\(-0.978\to-0.945\)). A regime variable that survives a 29-point shift in its own target is not an artifact of the baseline. It also passed its first out-of-sample test: it predicted llama3.3-70B's 37-point collapse at 128k, a point that did not exist when the boundary was established. This is the headline, and no prior work makes the claim. And the obvious statistical objection strengthens it: 24 configurations nested in six models are not 24 independent points, but partialling out model identity moves \(\rho\) from \(-0.941\) to \(-0.967\), and the relationship is perfectly monotone (\(-1.00\)) within every one of the six models.
  4. Context length is a phase variable, acting through \(\tau\). One model traverses GO → NARROW → STOP on context alone; \(\tau\) rises monotonically with \(L\) in all six models, extinguishing rungs from the bottom. A per-model verdict is ill-posed, which invalidates how this literature reports results — including our own prior versions.
  5. A cost model that forward-predicts the measured output-error gain to 0.79–1.34× on per-configuration medians across 24 configurations and two independently-configured campaigns, and independent of the corner definition. The allocation rule \(b_i^\star=\log_2 a_i+c\) is carried by this, not by the \(\tau/\ln2\) ladder identity it replaces (§3).
  6. The price of decode-time-available scoring, measured — and our own corner was an oracle. To be precise about whose error this was: H2O, SnapKV and StreamingLLM all score on observed attention, and we implement all three. The oracle corner — ranking by the current step’s true sensitivity \(a_i\lVert v_i-o\rVert\), which needs the very weights eviction exists to avoid computing — was our construction, and it silently set every band fraction this project reported through v7. Rebuilding it from real evictors moves the band +6.1 to +28.9 points in all 24 configurations and leaves no configuration STOP (14 GO, 10 NARROW). The generalisable result is the price: current-step truth is worth only 1.02–1.41× in error over a one-step-lagged score, and that advantage sits on diffuse heads — which matters for any method that scores once from the full prefill attention matrix and then freezes, since it never pays this cost while a decode-time re-budgeter does.
  7. Tail dependence is architectural and spans 36×. v7 proposed that the fractional eviction budget is mis-specified and that an absolute-support budget would restore the band. We built the test and it is false. \(K^\*\), the smallest keep-count landing within 10% of the full-budget corner, is 100% of the budget at every model and every context — there is no slack to reclaim, and a \(\kappa\,n_{95}\) budget at \(\kappa{=}4\) costs 1.9–7.4× the error. The diagnostic returned a better finding instead: \(K^\*/n_{95}\) spans 6.5 (mistral-7b) to 233 (llama3.3-70B), and is not ordered by support — llama3.3-70B has the smallest \(n_{95}\) in the study and the largest tail dependence. No global constant serves both; per-head keep-counts must come from measured support.
  8. Unbiased quantizers make bad indices. QJL's variance penalty puts its 1-bit tier above the cost of eviction and its 2-bit tier off the convex envelope — an independent explanation for the deployed backend omitting it.
  9. A methodological result about measuring attention. Gaussian-logit simulation gives a qualitatively wrong regime map because it locks concentration to \(\tau\). Separately, and more usefully to practitioners: three defects in our own pipeline each produced a plausible but wrong phase map, and the paper documents the validity gates that catch them — real-corpus enforcement, needle-retrieval verification, and a probe/chunking equivalence test.
  10. One cascade iteration suffices to resolve the circularity in \(b_i^\star\).

9. The three ways this still dies

  1. 128k is NARROW everywhere, and the trend points down. All three 128k-capable models now measured: 28.8%, 25.0%, 18.6% — every one NARROW, none STOP, but none GO either, and qwen3-30B sits 3.6 points above the kill line. Worse, the decay accelerates at that last octave rather than flattening: llama3.3-70B falls 61.9% → 25.0% in one doubling (−37 points), its \(\tau\) jumping 2.06 → 3.31 and \(n_{95}\) 117 → 4,854. If that acceleration continues past 128k — and nothing in the mechanism says it should stop — allocation helps mainly below ~64k, where the KV cache is least painful, and the systems contribution narrows to the phase diagram plus a router that mostly routes to corners. The science survives and is arguably strengthened by predicting its own collapse; the method's deployment reach does not.
  2. Nesting is not free at finite \(d\). Equitz–Cover is asymptotic and assumes optimal vector quantization; we use per-coordinate Lloyd-Max at \(d{=}128\) with three refinement boundaries. A 20% rate penalty removes the architecture's foundation.
  3. The gain is too small to matter. 1.5–4.4× routed on interior configurations is real but unspectacular, and it is an output-error gain, not an end-task or throughput gain. If it does not survive translation to RULER/AIME at matched bandwidth, the phase diagram remains true and the method remains uninteresting.
