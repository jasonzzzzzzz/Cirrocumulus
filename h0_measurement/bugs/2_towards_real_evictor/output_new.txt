Three findings
(a) E1's slack hypothesis is dead. K*/budget = 100.0% for every model at every ctx, 8K→128K. The corner genuinely needs its whole fractional budget everywhere. This was one ambiguous data point before; now it's 23 runs across 6 models. The "corner wins on slack" framing should be retired.

(b) κ=4 is wrong, and no single κ can work. K*/n95 ranges 6.5 (mistral) to 233 (llama33-70b) — a 36× spread across models, and it grows with ctx. With κ=4, abs saves 1.7–62× the bits but costs 1.9–7.4× the error. A global constant can't span that; K* itself is the per-head answer.

(c) The diffuse-head finding replicates everywhere. spearman(oracle_adv, n95) is positive in all 23 runs (+0.23 to +0.71); sharp quartile ~1.0–1.28×, diffuse quartile ~1.31–1.73×. why.md's "deflation is strongest for sharp models" is backwards — confirmed at scale, not just at 128K.

Bonus: accum (H2O) wins 78–93% of heads; window 6–17%, recency 1–5%. The min over three corners is barely stronger than H2O alone, so the verdict is robust to the evictor set.
