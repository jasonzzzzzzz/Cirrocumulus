A Phase Diagram for KV Cache Compression

Uniform quantization, per-token rate allocation, and token eviction are each optimal in a different regime. The boundary variable is derivable from the same cost comparison that generates the theory, it contains no context length, and context length is what moves models across it.

Thesis. Every KV compression method minimizes a reconstruction distortion. Attention does not consume keys; it consumes an output \(o=\sum_i a_iv_i\). Posed against output distortion, KV compression becomes a rate-allocation problem whose solution is reverse water-filling, with eviction recovered as the zero-rate case at derived cost \(c_0=1\). The three families are therefore one program — and the program tells you which of them to use.

What the completed H0 changed. The paper is no longer "our method wins", and it is no longer even "each method wins somewhere". It is: the regime is a function of (architecture, context length), the boundary variable falls out of the theory rather than being fitted, and one model traverses all three regimes as context grows. A per-model verdict — which every prior version of this document reported — is an ill-posed object. Along the way the measurement exposed a mis-specification in how the field defines eviction budgets that plausibly accounts for the corner's apparent dominance at 128k.

Every link in the chain is measured. \(\tau\) rises monotonically with \(L\) in all six models (within-model rank correlation 1.00). Rising \(\tau\) pushes \(\tau^2c_b\) past \(c_0=1\) for progressively higher rungs, so the dead-tier fraction climbs, the ladder is eaten from the bottom, and the optimum degenerates toward the two-tier solution that is eviction. Gain over uniform actually grows with context (23×→33×) — the interior is not getting worse in absolute terms; the eviction corner is catching up.

The second sweep separates two distinct causes of the same symptom. qwen3-30B moves barely at all (10.3% → 4.1% over 16× context) and carries 60–73% dead tiers at every length: it is pinned in the eviction corner by sink-dominated attention (median top-1 ≈ 0.5), architecturally, not because the context is long. llama3.1-8B's steep traversal is the opposite. Same corner, different mechanisms, distinguishable on the same axis.

The design question this exposes — and the first experiment of the next phase The standard eviction budget is mis-specified at long context. Every evictor in the literature, and our own corner baseline, keeps a fixed fraction of context: \(B\!\cdot\!L/b_{\max}\) tokens, linear in \(L\). Head support is not linear in \(L\). We measure \(n_{95}\propto L^{0.63}\) for llama3.1-8B, \(L^{0.73}\) for qwen3-30B, \(L^{0.76}\)–\(L^{0.92}\) for the others. The corner is therefore handed steadily more slack than any head can use — at \(B{=}3\) it keeps 15.8× a head's support at 4k and 70.8× at 64k.

This matters because it is exactly where we now lose. The eviction corner may be winning at 128k not because eviction is the right tool there, but because a fraction-of-\(L\) budget is an increasingly generous handicap. The clean test is an absolute-support budget — keep \(\max(\kappa\,n_{95},\,\text{floor})\) tokens rather than a fixed share — evaluated at matched total bits. If the 128k STOP verdicts survive it, they are real and the paper's scope narrows to mid-range context honestly. If they do not, the field has been comparing against a baseline that flatters itself precisely in the regime that motivates the work. Either outcome is publishable; we do not currently know which it is.

(llama3.3-70B is the exception that proves the mechanism: its \(n_{95}\) grows superlinearly, \(L^{1.81}\), reaching 4,808 tokens at 128k. It genuinely diffuses rather than being squeezed by the budget definition — and it is the only model still holding NARROW at 128k.)

3. What survived, what broke

Confirmed, and stronger than in v6 C4 — the ladder width is \(\tau/\ln2\). Across all 20 configurations, maximum error 1.4%, median 0.4% — tighter than the 3.4% reported on the corrupted campaign, over three times as many configurations and five octaves of context. A closed-form prediction landing this precisely is the strongest single piece of evidence in the paper.

The value term is decorative. Dropping \(\|v_i-o\|\) moves the ladder by at most 0.035 bits anywhere in the dataset. Allocation reduces to \(b_i^\star=\log_2 a_i+c\), reads only attention weights, and never touches the value cache. Built as an ablation against our own design; it simplified the method.

The linearization is far better than we thought. Predicted/measured gain now runs 0.80–1.35, against the 0.93–4.89 reported in v6. The 4.9× overstatement was an artifact of the corrupted cache, not a property of the theory. The linearized cost is now a usable first-order predictor — though every number in this proposal remains measured, never predicted.

The quantizer's temperature distortion is a universal constant. \(\alpha=0.940\)–\(0.944\) at 2 bits across all 20 configurations. One scalar, model- and context-independent, correctable offline.
Broken — and these belong in the paper, not the rebuttal H2 fails at the stated threshold. Spearman \(\rho\) between 2-bit scores and true logits over the top 1% is 0.52–0.77 against a 0.9 kill criterion. M3's order-preservation claim does not hold at 2 bits. The survivable reading is that ranking within the top 1% is a much harder test than separating head from tail — all the cascade's first pass needs — but the claim weakens to "the coarse pass identifies the head region, not the order within it", and the base layer moves to 3 bits.

The 1-bit tier is dead essentially everywhere. 95–100% of heads in every configuration. It should be removed from the offered set rather than allocated and then never chosen.

The band does narrow with context, and that is now measured rather than feared. At 128k only llama3.3-70B holds NARROW. Long context is the entire motivation for KV compression, so this is the central threat to the systems claim — and the absolute-support experiment above is the only thing that can distinguish "true" from "baseline artifact".

Three measurement defects each produced a plausible, wrong answer. A synthetic haystack (8 sentences tiled ~970×) made every head look diffuse; a top-left-aligned causal mask under chunked prefill corrupted the KV cache beyond the first 4,096 tokens of every prompt, which is what produced v6's entire results table; and the validity gate we built to catch the first was keyed on a bulk moment that a single needle cannot move, so it vetoed five good models. Each is documented with the bisect that isolated it and a regression test. We report this because a reviewer's first question about a 33,920-head attention study should be "how do you know your probe is right", and the answer is "we broke it three times and here is how we caught each".

4. The allocation theorem

With \(\hat s_i=s_i+\delta_i\), \(\operatorname{Var}(\delta_i)=\tau^2c_b\), and \(\partial o/\partial s_i=a_i(v_i-o)\), first-order output error is \(\sum_i a_i^2w_i^2\tau^2c_{b_i}\). Minimizing under \(\sum_ib_i\le B\) gives, above the water level,

$$b_i^\star=\log_2 a_i+c \qquad\text{(the } \|v_i-o\| \text{ factor is measured to be negligible, §2)}$$

and \(b_i^\star=0\) otherwise. Eviction's cost constant is derived, not chosen: dropping a set \(E\) and renormalizing costs exactly \(\sum_{i\in E}a_i^2\|o_R-v_i\|^2\), so \(c_0=1\) in absolute units and eviction sits on the same axis as every quantized tier. The comparison \(\tau^2c_b\) vs \(1\) is what generates the tier-extinction boundary of §1.2.

5. Architecture — the router is the method

v5 treated per-head routing as a safety valve. The completed measurement promotes it to the central component, and adds a requirement: the phase depends on the deployment context length, so the router must be indexed by (head, \(L\)) — but because the boundary variable is \(L\)-free, one calibration extrapolates instead of needing a sweep per deployment.

OFFLINE CALIBRATION (one forward pass; the H0 pipeline as shipped)
  per head: tau, per-tier sig2_b, dead-tier fraction  ->  phase assignment
  the test is derived, not fitted:  buy tier b only while tau^2 * c_b < c0 = 1

  ladder width tau/ln2 < ~2 b     -> UNIFORM bits, allocation disabled
  2-bit tier live, dead frac <45% -> SIEVE ladder, tiers from the convex envelope
  dead-2 fraction  >= ~45%        -> TWO-TIER evict + 8-bit
  1-bit tier                      -> never offered (dead in 95-100% of heads)

  tau rises monotonically with L, so a head can be re-phased for a new deployment
  context by re-evaluating the SAME test -- no re-measurement.

STORAGE  nested code, 8 b K + FP8 V = k8v4 budget, 2.0x vs BF16, layer-major

DECODE (band heads only)
  pass 0    all L tokens    3 b   -->  a-hat   --> allocate b* = log2(a_i) + c
  pass 1-3  shrinking sets  +1/+2/+2   --> refine
  tail      b* = 0          page centroids

  base layer is 3 b, not 2: H2 shows 2-bit scores do not rank within the head region.

Measured consequence at 32k: the router sends 20–59% of heads to the ladder across the four interior models, for routed gains of 1.39–2.29×, and correctly declines on the two Qwen3 models, handing ~94% of their heads to a corner — predominantly eviction, since 58–66% of them carry a dead 2-bit tier.

6. Contributions

  1. The objective is wrong. KV compression should minimize attention-output distortion, not key reconstruction distortion. Everything follows from this substitution.
  2. Eviction is quantization at zero rate; \(c_0=1\) is derived, not posited, putting two literatures on one axis for the first time.
  3. A phase diagram whose boundary variable is derived rather than fitted. The dead-tier fraction — heads with \(\tau^2c_b>c_0\) — predicts the productive band at \(\rho=-0.96\) across 20 configurations, six architectures and five octaves of context. It contains no \(L\). This is the headline, and no prior work makes the claim.
  4. Context length is a phase variable, acting through \(\tau\). One model traverses GO → NARROW → STOP on context alone; \(\tau\) rises monotonically with \(L\) in all six models, extinguishing rungs from the bottom. A per-model verdict is ill-posed, which invalidates how this literature reports results — including our own prior versions.
  5. \(b_i^\star=\log_2 a_i+c\), ladder width \(\tau/\ln2\), confirmed to within 1.4% on 33,920 heads.
  6. The fractional eviction budget is mis-specified. Evictors keep \(\propto L\) tokens; head support grows as \(L^{0.63-0.92}\). The corner's handicap therefore grows with context (15.8× → 70.8× a head's support), which plausibly accounts for its apparent dominance at 128k. We propose and test an absolute-support formulation.
  7. Unbiased quantizers make bad indices. QJL's variance penalty puts its 1-bit tier above the cost of eviction and its 2-bit tier off the convex envelope — an independent explanation for the deployed backend omitting it.
  8. A methodological result about measuring attention. Gaussian-logit simulation gives a qualitatively wrong regime map because it locks concentration to \(\tau\). Separately, and more usefully to practitioners: three defects in our own pipeline each produced a plausible but wrong phase map, and the paper documents the validity gates that catch them — real-corpus enforcement, needle-retrieval verification, and a probe/chunking equivalence test.
  9. One cascade iteration suffices to resolve the circularity in \(b_i^\star\).

7. Remaining experiments

E1 · absolute-support eviction budget · ~2 days · HIGHEST PRIORITY Replace the corner's \(B\!\cdot\!L/b_{\max}\) budget with \(\max(\kappa\,n_{95},\text{floor})\) at matched total bits, and re-evaluate the sweep. This decides whether the 128k STOP verdicts are a fact about attention or an artifact of how the field defines eviction budgets. It is the single highest-information experiment remaining, it needs no new measurement infrastructure, and both outcomes are publishable.
E2 · practical evictor · ~2 days The eviction corner is an oracle ranking by true sensitivity; real H2O/SnapKV rank on past attention. The pipeline already carries a lagged-attention score. This widens the band wherever the oracle was flattering itself, and the oracle's advantage is largest exactly at long context, where we currently lose.
E3 · 3-bit base layer · ~2 days  ·  E4 · nested coding overhead · ~1 week Re-run H2 at 3 bits to fix the base layer. Then measure whether the nested 3+1+2+2 code costs rate against a monolithic 8-bit code; kill if the gap exceeds ~10%.
E5 · complete the sweep grid · ~2 days Two models are swept end to end; the \(\tau(L)\) mechanism is monotone in all six but the full traversal is measured on two. Sweeping the remaining four closes the central claim's evidence base cheaply, and tests whether the sharp boundary really sits near 45–50% dead tiers.
E6 · GQA union · ~1 week  ·  E7 · kernel, iso-budget, vs dense FP8 · ~4 weeks Restrict scope if the \(G{=}8\) union exceeds 3× the single-head footprint. Kill the systems claim if TPOT at 128k does not beat dense FP8 at equal accuracy.

9. The three ways this still dies

  1. The band is genuinely gone at 128k. If E1 shows the STOP verdicts survive an absolute-support budget, then allocation helps mainly below ~32k — where the KV cache is least painful — and the systems contribution collapses to the phase diagram plus a router that mostly routes to corners. The science survives; the method does not.
  2. Nesting is not free at finite \(d\). Equitz–Cover is asymptotic and assumes optimal vector quantization; we use per-coordinate Lloyd-Max at \(d{=}128\) with three refinement boundaries. A 20% rate penalty removes the architecture's foundation.
  3. The gain is too small to matter. 1.4–3.1× routed on interior configurations is real but unspectacular, and it is an output-error gain, not an end-task or throughput gain. If it does not survive translation to RULER/AIME at matched bandwidth, the phase diagram remains true and the method remains uninteresting.
