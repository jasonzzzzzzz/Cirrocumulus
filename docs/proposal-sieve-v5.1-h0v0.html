A Phase Diagram for KV Cache Compression

Uniform quantization, per-token rate allocation, and token eviction are each optimal in a different regime of attention concentration. The boundaries are derivable from one equation, and we measure them on 10,240 heads.

Thesis. Every KV compression method minimizes a reconstruction distortion. Attention does not consume keys; it consumes an output \(o=\sum_i a_iv_i\). Posed against output distortion, KV compression becomes a rate-allocation problem whose solution is reverse water-filling, with eviction recovered as the zero-rate case. The three families are therefore one program — and the program tells you which of them to use.

What H0 changed. The measurement was meant to answer "does allocation pay?". It answered a better question. Allocation beats both corners on 38% of all heads measured (53% on the median model, 2.06× geometric-mean routed gain), and the heads where it fails do so for two distinct and predictable reasons at opposite ends of a single axis. The paper is no longer "our method wins"; it is a map of when each method wins, with our method owning the middle.

1.1 The regime variable is effective support, not logit spread

v5 predicted, from simulation, that the gain peaks near \(\tau\approx1.25\) and vanishes by \(\tau\approx2\). Measurement contradicts this outright. The model closest to the predicted optimum — llama3.3-70B at \(\tau=1.36\) — is second-worst at 28%. The four models in the supposedly dead zone (\(\tau=2.2\)–\(3.2\)) return 46–85%. And \(\tau\) has no discriminating power at the sharp end: two models within 0.02 of each other in \(\tau\) differ by a factor of four in outcome.

What does separate them is effective support \(\varphi = n_{95}/L\), the fraction of the context carrying 95% of the attention mass:

$$\varphi > 0.5 \;\Rightarrow\; \textbf{uniform},\qquad 0.1 \le \varphi \le 0.5 \;\Rightarrow\; \textbf{allocation},\qquad \varphi < 0.1 \;\Rightarrow\; \textbf{eviction}.$$

This rule classifies all six models correctly with no fitted parameters, and it has a reading that needs no theory: when almost every token matters, spend bits evenly; when almost none does, throw them away; in between, allocate. The reason the simulation got this wrong is instructive and belongs in the paper — with Gaussian logits, \(\varphi\) is a deterministic function of \(\tau\), so the two cannot be told apart. Real attention logits are heavy-tailed, and \(\tau\) (a second moment) decouples from top-1 weight (an extreme-value statistic). qwen3-30B has \(\tau=3.05\) yet a top-1 weight of 0.20 and \(\varphi=2.6\%\): a few enormous outliers over an otherwise ordinary spread.

2. What survived, what broke

Confirmed C4 — the ladder width is \(\tau/\ln2\). Predicted vs measured: 3.19/3.09, 4.43/4.28, 3.38/3.32, 4.57/4.50, 1.96/1.96, 4.40/4.41. Maximum error 3.4%, across three architectures, two attention variants and a 20× parameter range. A closed-form prediction landing this precisely on production models is the strongest single piece of evidence in the paper.

The value term is negligible. On all six models the ladder computed from \(a_i\|v_i-o\|\) lies on the diagonal against the ladder from \(a_i\) alone. The allocation rule simplifies to \(b_i^\star=\log_2 a_i + c\) — which means allocation needs only attention weights, never the value cache. This was built as an ablation to test one of our own design choices, and it says the choice was unnecessary.

Attention sinks are a non-issue. \(\tau\) and \(\tau\) excluding the four sink positions agree to two decimals on every model. A predicted confound, eliminated.

The quantizer's temperature distortion is a universal constant. \(\alpha \approx 0.94\) at 2 bits on all six models (0.940–0.943). One scalar, model-independent, correctable offline.
Broken — and these must be in the paper, not the rebuttal H2 fails at the stated threshold. Spearman \(\rho\) between 2-bit scores and true logits over the top 1% is 0.47–0.56 on five of six models (mistral 0.88). The kill criterion was \(\rho<0.9\). M3's order-preservation claim does not hold at 2 bits. The survivable reading is that ranking within the top 1% is a much harder test than separating the head from the tail — which is all the cascade's first pass needs — but the claim must be weakened to "the coarse pass identifies the head region, not the order within it", and the base layer probably needs 3 bits rather than 2.

The linearization is badly inaccurate. Predicted/measured gain runs 0.93–4.89. It is accurate on the sharpest model (0.93) and overstates by ~5× on the most diffuse. The theorem remains the right allocation rule; it is not a usable predictor of the gain. Every number in this proposal is measured, never predicted, and the paper must say so explicitly.

The two largest models are the two worst. 28% and 15%, against 46–85% for the four smaller ones. With \(n=6\) this could be coincidence — the two failures have opposite mechanisms, which argues against a single scale effect — but it is the most uncomfortable pattern in the data for a paper about production serving.

Context length is confounded with model identity. The three 128k runs return 60%, 28%, 15%; the three shorter runs return 85%, 62%, 46%. If the band narrows with context, that is bad news, because long context is the entire motivation. Sweeping one model across 8k–128k is now the single most important follow-up and costs under a day.

3. The allocation theorem

With \(\hat s_i=s_i+\delta_i\), \(\operatorname{Var}(\delta_i)=\tau^2c_b\), and \(\partial o/\partial s_i=a_i(v_i-o)\), first-order output error is \(\sum_i a_i^2w_i^2\tau^2c_{b_i}\). Minimizing under \(\sum_ib_i\le B\) gives, above the water level,

$$b_i^\star=\log_2 a_i+c \qquad\text{(the } \|v_i-o\| \text{ factor is measured to be negligible, §2)}$$

and \(b_i^\star=0\) otherwise. Eviction's cost constant is derived, not chosen: dropping a set \(E\) and renormalizing costs exactly \(\sum_{i\in E}a_i^2\|o_R-v_i\|^2\), so \(c_0=1\) in absolute units and eviction sits on the same axis as every quantized tier. The comparison \(\tau^2c_b\) vs \(1\) is what generates the tier-extinction boundary of §1.2.

4. Architecture — the router is now the method

v5 treated per-head routing as a safety valve. The phase diagram promotes it to a first-class component: a model is a mixture of phases, and the compressor's job is to identify each head's phase and apply the locally optimal scheme.

OFFLINE CALIBRATION (one forward pass; the H0 pipeline as shipped)
  per head: phi = n95/L, tau, dead-tier fraction  ->  phase assignment

  phi > 0.5                     -> UNIFORM bits, allocation disabled
  0.1 <= phi <= 0.5             -> SIEVE ladder, width tau/ln2, tiers from the envelope
  phi < 0.1  or  >25% tiers dead-> TWO-TIER evict + 8-bit

STORAGE  nested code, 8 b K + FP8 V = k8v4 budget, 2.0x vs BF16, layer-major

DECODE (band heads only)
  pass 0    all L tokens    3 b   -->  a-hat   --> allocate b* = log2(a_i) + c
  pass 1-3  shrinking sets  +1/+2/+2   --> refine
  tail      b* = 0          page centroids

  base layer is 3 b, not 2: H2 shows 2-bit scores do not rank within the head region.

Measured consequence: on the four GO models the router sends 46–85% of heads to the ladder and the rest to a corner, for a routed gain of 1.77–4.15×. On llama3.3-70B it sends 72% of heads to uniform, and the method correctly declines to act.

5. Contributions

  1. The objective is wrong. KV compression should minimize attention-output distortion, not key reconstruction distortion. Everything follows from this substitution.
  2. A phase diagram. Uniform quantization, rate allocation and eviction are optimal in three adjacent regimes of effective support, with boundaries at \(\varphi\approx0.5\) and \(\varphi\approx0.1\). Six production models classify correctly with no fitted parameters. This is the paper's headline and it is a claim no prior work makes.
  3. Both boundaries are derived from one equation — ladder collapse at the diffuse end, tier extinction at the sharp end, each from \(\tau^2c_b\) vs the derived \(c_0=1\).
  4. \(b_i^\star=\log_2 a_i+c\), with ladder width \(\tau/\ln2\), confirmed on real models to within 3.4%.
  5. Eviction is quantization at zero rate; \(c_0=1\) derived, unifying two literatures.
  6. Unbiased quantizers make bad indices. QJL's variance penalty puts its 1-bit tier above the cost of eviction and its 2-bit tier off the convex envelope, costing ~2 rungs of ladder at every \(\tau\) — an independent explanation for the deployed backend omitting it.
  7. A methodological result: Gaussian-logit simulation gives a qualitatively wrong regime map, because it locks \(\varphi\) to \(\tau\). Real logits are heavy-tailed and the two decouple. Anyone modelling attention statistics analytically should know this.
  8. One cascade iteration suffices to resolve the circularity in \(b_i^\star\).

6. Remaining experiments

E1 · context-length sweep · ~1 day · HIGHEST PRIORITY One model, 8k → 128k, measuring \(\varphi\) and band fraction. Separates model identity from context length and tells us whether the productive band survives at the lengths that motivate the work. If the band collapses with length, the paper narrows to mid-range context and the systems claim weakens sharply.
E2 · practical evictor · ~2 days The eviction corner here is an oracle ranking by true sensitivity. Real H2O/SnapKV rank on past attention. The pipeline already carries a lagged-attention score; running it widens the band wherever the oracle was flattering itself, and is the cheapest available upside.
E3 · 3-bit base layer · ~2 days  ·  E4 · nested coding overhead · ~1 week Re-run H2 at 3 bits to fix the base layer. Then measure whether the nested 3+1+2+2 code costs rate against a monolithic 8-bit code; kill if the gap exceeds ~10%.
E5 · GQA union · ~1 week  ·  E6 · kernel, iso-budget, vs dense FP8 · ~4 weeks Restrict scope if the \(G{=}8\) union exceeds 3× the single-head footprint. Kill the systems claim if TPOT at 128k does not beat dense FP8 at equal accuracy.

8. The three ways this still dies

  1. The band shrinks with context length. The three 128k runs are the three lowest band fractions. If that is causal rather than coincidental, the method works best exactly where the KV cache is not yet a problem. E1 settles it in a day and it runs next.
  2. Nesting is not free at finite \(d\). Equitz–Cover is asymptotic and assumes optimal vector quantization; we use per-coordinate Lloyd-Max at \(d{=}128\) with three refinement boundaries. A 20% rate penalty removes the architecture's foundation.
  3. The gain is too small to matter. 2.06× routed is a real but unspectacular number, and it is an output-error gain, not yet an end-task or throughput gain. If it does not survive translation to RULER/AIME accuracy at matched bandwidth, the phase diagram remains true and the method remains uninteresting.
