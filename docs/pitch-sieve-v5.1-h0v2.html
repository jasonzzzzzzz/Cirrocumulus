ICLR 2027 · project pitch · v5 · 24 model×context points, honest eviction corner, 8k–128k

KV cache compression has a
phase diagram, and context length moves you across it.

Quantize every token evenly, or throw most of them away — the field has argued between these for three years. They are corner solutions to one rate-allocation problem, each optimal in its own regime. We derive the boundary variable, show that a single model crosses all three phases as context grows from 4k to 128k, and identify a mis-specification in how eviction budgets are defined that makes the corner look better than it is at long context.

Abstract

what the paper says

Serving long-context LLMs is bounded by the KV cache, and every method that shrinks it — quantization or token eviction — minimizes key reconstruction error. We show this is the wrong distortion measure. Posed against attention-output error, KV compression becomes a rate-allocation problem whose solution is reverse water-filling: the optimal budget for a token is linear in the log of its attention weight, and eviction emerges as the zero-rate case of the same equation, with cost constant \(c_0=1\) derived rather than posited. Comparing \(\tau^2c_b\) against that 1 yields both a phase diagram and its boundary variable: the fraction of heads whose low tiers are extinct. We measure it on 24 model×context configurations, six architectures, 8k–128k — six production models at matched context, plus two full sweeps from 4k to 128k. The dead-tier fraction predicts the outcome at Spearman \(\rho=-0.94\) pooled and \(-0.97\) with model identity partialled out — it is stronger within a model than across the set, so the nesting of 24 configurations in six models works in its favour — and the linearized cost model forward-predicts the measured output-error gain to 0.79–1.34×. The sweeps show context length is itself a phase variable — llama3.1-8B goes GO → NARROW → STOP with nothing changed but \(L\) — and — with the eviction corner rebuilt from real evictors rather than an oracle — that no configuration is STOP. A per-head keep-count cannot be a constant: tail dependence \(K^\*/n_{95}\) spans 37× across the model set and is not ordered by support.

The problem

why the field is stuck

At 128k+ context the KV cache dominates memory and decode bandwidth. Two families attack it, they disagree, and both are right — in different places, which nobody had established:

FamilyAllocatesHow it fails
Eviction
H2O, SnapKV		{0, 16} bitsDrops tokens a future query will need. Missing one critical key produces sharp error spikes in long-output reasoning.
Quantization
KIVI, QJL, TurboQuant		{b, b} bitsAggressive variants lose up to 20 points on AIME25 and ~30% relative on 256k retrieval, and pay 10–68% latency for dequantization. FP8 beats all of them on the Pareto frontier.

Both fix the allocation a priori, then optimize a proxy objective — how faithfully a key is reproduced — that attention never evaluates.

Attention does not consume keys. It consumes \(o=\sum_i a_i v_i\). A key carrying 0.4 of the mass and a key carrying \(10^{-6}\) are not equally worth storing, and no reconstruction metric can tell them apart.

Swap the distortion measure and the answer is classical. With \(\operatorname{Var}(\delta_i)=\tau^2c_b\) and \(\partial o/\partial s_i = a_i(v_i-o)\), minimizing \(\mathbb{E}\|\Delta o\|^2\) under \(\sum_i b_i\le B\) is reverse water-filling:

$$b_i^\star=\log_2 a_i + c\,,\qquad b_i^\star = 0 \ \text{ below the water level.}$$

Dropping a set and renormalizing costs exactly \(\sum_{i\in E}a_i^2\|o_R-v_i\|^2\), so eviction's cost constant is \(c_0=1\), derived. For the first time eviction and quantization are the same quantity on the same axis — and comparing \(\tau^2c_b\) against that 1 is what produces the phase diagram.

The Qwen3 family is pinned in the eviction corner at every context length — 60–73% dead tiers at 8k as readily as at 128k — because its attention is sink-dominated (median top-1 weight ≈ 0.5). two predictions we got wrong, and the second one we caught ourselves v5 indexed the regime by \(\tau\). Wrong: \(\tau\) alone reaches only \(\rho=-0.74\), because two models can share a \(\tau\) and differ fourfold in outcome. v6 replaced it with effective support \(\varphi=n_{95}/L\). Also wrong, and worse — on valid data \(\varphi\) correlates with the outcome at \(+0.26\), the wrong sign, because \(\varphi\) divides by \(L\) and so conflates the architecture with the context length it was measured at. The variable that works was in the theory the whole time: push \(\tau\) through the tier-extinction test \(\tau^2c_b\) vs \(c_0=1\) and the result is \(L\)-free, derived, and predicts 20 points at \(-0.96\). We report this because the correction is the finding: an index chosen for fit gets overturned by the next campaign; one derived from the cost comparison does not.

Context length is a phase variable

the result that reframes the paper

The 128k row is complete on all three models that reach it — 28.8%, 25.0%, 18.6%. All NARROW; none STOP. And it delivered the study's sharpest result: llama3.3-70B, the best model at every other length, loses 37 points in the final octave (61.9% → 25.0%), with \(\tau\) jumping 2.06 → 3.31 and \(n_{95}\) 117 → 4,854. The phase variable predicted it — dead-2 goes 12.0% → 41.4% — on a point that did not exist when the boundary was drawn. That is the diagram's first out-of-sample test.

The architecture ranking is not stable across context. The best model in the study at 8k–64k is the second worst at 128k. That is the thesis, demonstrated at its own expense.

−10.0 points of band per context doubling, monotone across all five octaves — and the same slope on every model in the set (llama3.3-70B −10.3, qwen1.5-MoE −9.0, qwen3-8B −6.9, mistral-7b −5.2, qwen3-30B −3.5). Context is the steepest axis in the study, steeper than architecture. The honest corner does not flatten it; it moves the intercept up by 15–29 points, which is the difference between STOP and a shippable regime.

The causal chain is fully mechanistic and every link is measured. \(\tau\) rises monotonically with \(L\) in all six models (rank correlation 1.00 within every model). Rising \(\tau\) pushes \(\tau^2c_b\) past \(c_0=1\) for progressively higher rungs, so the dead-tier fraction climbs, the ladder loses rungs from the bottom, and the optimum degenerates toward the two-tier solution that is eviction. Gain over the eviction corner decays 1.85× → 1.03× in lockstep.

A per-model verdict is not a well-posed object. The router must condition on (architecture, context) — and the phase diagram is what makes that a lookup rather than a re-measurement.

the design question this exposes — and it favours us The standard eviction budget is mis-specified at long context. Every evictor, ours included as the corner baseline, keeps a fixed fraction of the context: \(B\!\cdot\!L/b_{\max}\) tokens, linear in \(L\). But a head's actual support is strongly sublinear — we measure \(n_{95}\propto L^{0.63}\) for llama3.1-8B, \(L^{0.73}\) for qwen3-30B, \(L^{0.76-0.92}\) for the rest. So as context grows the corner is handed exponentially more slack than the head can use: 15.8× the head's support at 4k, 70.8× at 64k. The eviction corner is not winning at 128k because eviction is good there; it is winning because a fraction-of-\(L\) budget becomes an increasingly generous handicap. An absolute-support budget — keep \(\max(\kappa\,n_{95}, \text{floor})\) tokens rather than a fixed share — is the natural formulation, and it is exactly the comparison that decides whether the STOP verdicts at 128k are real. This is the first experiment in the next phase.

The router is the product, and the phase diagram is what makes it cheap. A model is a mixture of phases, and which phase a head is in depends on the deployment context length as much as on the architecture. The compressor's job is to read each head's phase from a one-pass offline calibration — the same forward-pass hook that produced this data — and apply the locally optimal scheme. Because the boundary variable is derived and \(L\)-free, that calibration extrapolates across context lengths instead of needing a fresh sweep per deployment.

Two properties make this deployable rather than merely true. The value term \(\|v_i-o\|\) is measured to be decorative (ladder shifts by at most 0.035 bits when it is dropped), so allocation reads attention weights and never touches the value cache. And the floor is 1.0×: outside the band the router hands the head to the corner that owns it, so the method declines rather than loses.

Novel claims

each falsifiable; five now tested on production models

  1. The objective is wrong. KV compression should minimize attention-output distortion, not key reconstruction distortion. Everything follows from this substitution. theory
  2. Eviction is quantization at zero rate. \(c_0=1\) is derived, not posited; H2O and KIVI become corner solutions to one program, on one axis, for the first time. confirmed
  3. KV compression has three phases, and the boundary variable is derived from the same cost comparison — the fraction of heads with an extinct 2-bit tier, \(\tau^2c_2>c_0\). It contains no \(L\) and predicts the band at \(\rho=-0.945\) over 24 configurations spanning six architectures and five octaves of context. measured Reported with model identity partialled out (\(\rho=-0.967\), and \(-1.00\) within every one of the six models), because 24 configurations nested in six models are not 24 independent points.
  4. Context length is a phase variable, acting through \(\tau\). \(\tau\) rises monotonically with \(L\) in every model, extinguishing rungs from the bottom; one model crosses GO → NARROW → STOP on the sweep alone. Per-model verdicts are ill-posed. measured
  5. Optimal precision is logarithmic in attention mass, \(b_i^\star=\log_2 a_i+c\), and the cost model it comes from forward-predicts the measured output-error gain to 0.79–1.34× across all 24 configurations. (The ladder width \(\tau/\ln2\) is an algebraic identity, not evidence — appendix consistency check only.) measured
  6. Tail dependence is architectural, and no fixed budget serves a model set. We proposed that eviction’s fixed-fraction budget was mis-specified and that an absolute-support budget would restore the band. We built the test and it is false: \(K^\*\), the smallest keep-count landing within 10% of the full-budget corner, is 100% of the budget at every model and every context — there is no slack to reclaim. What the diagnostic returned instead is better: \(K^\*/n_{95}\) spans 37× (5.8 for mistral-7b to 214.7 for llama3.3-70B) and is not ordered by support — the 70B has the smallest \(n_{95}\) in the study and the deepest tail. Per-head keep-counts must come from measured support, not a constant. proposed, tested, refuted — and replaced
  7. The value term is decorative. The \(\|v_i-o\|\) factor moves the ladder by at most 0.035 bits, so allocation needs only attention weights and never touches the value cache. We built this as an ablation against our own design and it simplified the method. measured
  8. Unbiased quantizers make bad indices. QJL's variance penalty pushes its 1-bit tier above the cost of eviction and its 2-bit tier off the convex envelope — an independent explanation for the deployed backend deleting it. confirmed
  9. Scale does not hurt; context does. At matched context the 70B is the best model — 92.6% at 8k and 75.4% at 32k against a real evictor (82.1% / 61.4% against the oracle). The opposite trend we reported in v6 was context length in disguise. reversed by matched-ctx data
  10. Attention measurement is a methodological minefield, and we have the receipts. Three separate defects each silently produced a plausible, wrong regime map: a synthetic haystack, a top-left-aligned causal mask under chunked prefill, and a validity gate keyed on a bulk moment that a single needle cannot move. Each is documented with the bisect that found it. found, fixed, recorded

A framework that only reorganizes known results gets scored as a survey. This one forward-predicts an exactly-recomputed error ratio to within −21%/+34% across 24 model×context configurations, derives its own boundary variable rather than fitting one, and has now discarded two candidate indices — including one we published in the previous revision — because the derived quantity beat them both.

What is not yet settled

stated plainly, because reviewers will find these

  • The band narrows with context, and that is now measured rather than feared. Against a real evictor the 128k row reads 28.8% / 25.0% / 18.6% on the three models that reach it — all NARROW, none STOP. But none is GO either, and the decay accelerates in the last octave rather than flattening: llama3.3-70B falls 61.9% → 25.0% in one doubling. If that continues past 128k, allocation helps mainly below ~64k.
  • The interior is still scored by an oracle, even though the corner is not. We demoted the eviction corner to lagged attention, which removed every STOP verdict — but the interior still water-fills on current-step sensitivity. The symmetric cell (both sides lagged) is the last asymmetry in the comparison and is the next experiment.
  • 2-bit scores do not rank within the head region. Spearman \(\rho\) is 0.52–0.77 against a 0.9 threshold, so the coarse pass separates head from tail but does not order the head. The base layer moves to 3 bits.
  • The sharp boundary is populated but not pinned. With 20 points the STOP threshold sits somewhere near 45–50% dead tiers; we draw the region, not a line.
  • Output error is not end-task accuracy. Lower output error must survive translation to RULER and AIME at matched bandwidth before the systems claim means anything. This remains the largest single gap between the paper and a deployment argument.
  • Two models were swept, not six. The \(\tau(L)\) mechanism is monotone in all six, but the full GO→STOP traversal is measured on llama3.1-8B and qwen3-30B only.

Roughly six weeks: absolute-support eviction budget and practical evictor (4 days, and the first could move every 128k verdict), 3-bit base layer and nested-coding overhead (10 days), GQA union cost (1 week), then the kernel and the iso-budget ablation against dense FP8 on hardware (4 weeks) — with a kill gate at each stage.
