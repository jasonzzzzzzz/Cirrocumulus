## Findings

**1. Exact values on the selected rows help (Q1).** `tail4x_v4` − `tail4_v4`, KL/token:
- lower in 13 of 17 groups, with the interval below 0 in 10 of them (Llama: −0.0001 to −0.0022;
  Qwen harder tracking −0.0104);
- about equal in 3 (Qwen retrieval twice and Qwen RULER multivalue, all |Δ| < 0.00005);
- higher in 1: Qwen harder multivalue, +0.0009 [0.0000, 0.0024], driven by a single unit (see 4).

This confirms R5's finding 3: 4-bit values on the read rows were what kept the tail at FP8's level.
The cost is traffic 0.344 instead of 0.297.

- **`tail4x_v4`** is below FP8 in 7 of 11 groups and equal in 2 (Llama aggregation; Qwen retrieval,

- **The 3-bit tail** is below FP8 on Llama retrieval, multivalue, tracking and ICL, and on Qwen
  retrieval. It is 1.8–6× FP8's KL on the rest.
- **2-bit tails** do not reach FP8: 1.9–70× FP8's KL in every family but retrieval, where every arm
  is ≤ 0.0003. Even so, they beat dense 2-bit by 0.04–1.0 nats/token (Q2b).

**3. The read fraction and selection matter only for coarse tails (Q3, Q4).**
- **Read fraction (2-bit tail):** 1/16 is worse than 1/8 and 1/4 is better, as Lemma 3 predicts.
  At equal traffic (0.344), though, `tail2x_v2` at 1/4 loses to `tail4x_v4` at 1/8 in every family
  but Llama retrieval (a tie): a finer tier beats more exact rows.
- **Selection:** with a 2-bit tail it matters again, in opposite directions:
  - on Llama the static oracle's rows beat the vote's (re-ranking −0.108, ICL −0.012);
  - on Qwen they lose (+0.010 to +0.16; accuracy 0 to −0.55).

  At 4 bits this question is moot: the tier is good enough that the vote's choice barely moves KL
  (R5).

**4. The draft R6 margin is tighter than FP itself can meet on Qwen.** The draft test is
"KL/token(design) < 1.25 × FP8's + 2e-4". It was applied to the R5.3 arms per model × family,
pooling cells:
- No arm passes the draft rule (point estimate in every family, upper bound in ≥ 3/4).
- `tail4x_v4` scores 10/11 on point estimates and 9/11 on upper bounds. That is exactly what
  `fp_noise` (FP rerun) scores: FP itself fails Qwen aggregation (noise 0.0040 vs FP8's 0.0029) and
  Qwen tracking's upper bound.
- `tail4x_v4`'s one point miss, Qwen multivalue, is a single sensitive unit (prompt 9422). Every
  arm's KL spikes there: FP8 0.0020, `tail4x_v4` 0.0049, `tail4_v4` 0.0008. The other seven units are
  at the 1e-5 level, and `tail4x_v4`'s accuracy there is 1.0 (`tail4_v4` and `tail3x_v3` lose 0.04).
- At 1.5 × FP8 + 5e-4:
  - `fp_noise` passes 11/11 on point estimates and 10/11 on upper bounds;
  - `tail4x_v4` passes 11/11 and 9/11. Its upper-bound misses are re-ranking (5 units) and Qwen
    multivalue.

**5. Accuracy.** On FP-correct units, `tail4x_v4` is never below FP8: Llama 0 units lower, 1
higher (43 units); Qwen 0 and 0 (36 units).

## What this means for R6 (plan.md "R6 (draft)")

- **The arm:** `tail4x_v4` at 1/8. It is the only R5.3 arm at FP8's level in every family, and it is
  as close to FP as FP's own rerun.
- **The margin needs a calibration check.** FP (`fp_noise`) must pass the confirmatory test, or the
  test measures noise. 1.25 × FP8 + 2e-4 fails that check on Qwen; 1.5 × FP8 + 5e-4 passes it. This
  is a proposal for the user to accept or change before R6 is frozen, not an adopted rule.
- **Sizes at 1.5 × FP8 + 5e-4 for `tail4x_v4`** (`findings/R5_sizing.md`; normal approximation, R5.3's
  units):
  - Llama: 5–22 units per family, re-ranking 96;
  - Qwen: 5–12 per family, multivalue 94.

  With a floor of 30 per family (re-ranking and Qwen multivalue at 100), that is about 280 units for
  Llama and 190 for Qwen. Outside aggregation those hold ≈ 180 and ≈ 155 FP-correct units, enough
  for the accuracy floor at −0.02 (150). At R5's seconds per unit, GPU time is about 8 h and 3.5 h
  for six arms, plus model loads.
- **Correction to the earlier sizing.** It paired R5.3's arms with FP8 rows from R5's blocks:
  same prompts, different processes. `size_confirm_r5.py` now pairs within a block only, and draws
  each design from the run that contains it. The numbers above use the corrected script.
