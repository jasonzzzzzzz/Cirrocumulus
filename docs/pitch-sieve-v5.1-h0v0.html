ICLR 2027 · project pitch · v3 · measured on 6 production models

KV cache compression has a
phase diagram. Nobody drew it.

Quantize every token evenly, or throw most of them away — the field has argued between these for three years. They are corner solutions to one rate-allocation problem, each optimal in its own regime. We derive the boundaries, measure them on 10,240 attention heads, and occupy the middle.

Abstract

what the paper says

Serving long-context LLMs is bounded by the KV cache, and every method that shrinks it — quantization or token eviction — minimizes key reconstruction error. We show this is the wrong distortion measure. Posed against attention-output error, KV compression becomes a rate-allocation problem whose solution is reverse water-filling: the optimal budget for a token is linear in the log of its attention weight, and eviction emerges as the zero-rate case of the same equation. This yields a phase diagram: uniform quantization, per-token allocation and eviction are each optimal over a distinct range of attention concentration, with boundaries derived from the same cost comparison. We measure the diagram on 10,240 heads across six production models at 32k–128k context. It classifies every model correctly with no fitted parameters; the interior wins on 38% of all heads and up to 85% of a given model, and the two boundary predictions — ladder width \(\tau/\ln2\) and low-tier extinction — hold to within 3.4% and are directly observed.

The problem

why the field is stuck

At 128k+ context the KV cache dominates memory and decode bandwidth. Two families attack it, they disagree, and both are right — in different places, which nobody had established:

FamilyAllocatesHow it fails
Eviction
H2O, SnapKV		{0, 16} bitsDrops tokens a future query will need. Missing one critical key produces sharp error spikes in long-output reasoning.
Quantization
KIVI, QJL, TurboQuant		{b, b} bitsAggressive variants lose up to 20 points on AIME25 and ~30% relative on 256k retrieval, and pay 10–68% latency for dequantization. FP8 beats all of them on the Pareto frontier.

Both fix the allocation a priori, then optimize a proxy objective — how faithfully a key is reproduced — that attention never evaluates.

Attention does not consume keys. It consumes \(o=\sum_i a_i v_i\). A key carrying 0.4 of the mass and a key carrying \(10^{-6}\) are not equally worth storing, and no reconstruction metric can tell them apart.

Swap the distortion measure and the answer is classical. With \(\operatorname{Var}(\delta_i)=\tau^2c_b\) and \(\partial o/\partial s_i = a_i(v_i-o)\), minimizing \(\mathbb{E}\|\Delta o\|^2\) under \(\sum_i b_i\le B\) is reverse water-filling:

$$b_i^\star=\log_2 a_i + c\,,\qquad b_i^\star = 0 \ \text{ below the water level.}$$

Dropping a set and renormalizing costs exactly \(\sum_{i\in E}a_i^2\|o_R-v_i\|^2\), so eviction's cost constant is \(c_0=1\), derived. For the first time eviction and quantization are the same quantity on the same axis — and comparing \(\tau^2c_b\) against that 1 is what produces the phase diagram.

The two failures fail for opposite reasons, and the theory names both in advance. llama3.3-70B is too diffuse: its ladder is 1.96 bits wide and uniform is already almost right. qwen3-30B is too sharp: 77% of its heads have a dead 2-bit tier — the low rungs cost more than eviction — so the optimum degenerates to two tiers, and two tiers is an evictor. Against 1%, 3%, 5% and 8% dead-tier fractions for the models in the band, that single number is the cleanest diagnostic in the dataset.

a prediction we got wrong, and what it taught us Our simulation predicted the gain would peak at \(\tau\approx1.25\) and vanish by \(\tau\approx2\). Measurement says otherwise: the model nearest that optimum is second-worst, and four models in the supposedly dead zone return 46–85%. Worse, \(\tau\) has no discriminating power at all at the sharp end — qwen3-30B and qwen1.5-MoE differ by 0.02 in \(\tau\) and by a factor of four in outcome. The cause is that Gaussian-logit simulation locks \(\varphi\) to \(\tau\); real attention logits are heavy-tailed and the two decouple. qwen3-30B has an ordinary \(\tau=3.05\) alongside a top-1 weight of 0.20. This is a methodological finding in its own right, and it is why H0 had to be run on real models rather than argued from a generative model of attention.

The router is the product. A model is a mixture of phases, so the compressor's job is to identify each head's phase from a one-pass offline calibration and apply the locally optimal scheme. That is a deployable artifact independent of whether our ladder is the best possible interior code — and it is something practitioners can use on day one, since the calibration is the same forward-pass hook that produced this data.

Novel claims

each falsifiable; five now tested on production models

  1. The objective is wrong. KV compression should minimize attention-output distortion, not key reconstruction distortion. Everything follows from this substitution. theory
  2. KV compression has three phases, selected by effective support \(\varphi=n_{95}/L\): uniform above 0.5, allocation between 0.1 and 0.5, eviction below 0.1. Six models classify correctly with no fitted parameters. measured
  3. Both boundaries follow from one equation. Ladder collapse at the diffuse end, low-tier extinction at the sharp end — each from comparing \(\tau^2c_b\) with the derived eviction cost \(c_0=1\). measured
  4. Optimal precision is logarithmic in attention mass, \(b_i^\star=\log_2 a_i+c\), with ladder width exactly \(\tau/\ln2\) bits — confirmed to within 3.4% on every model. measured
  5. Eviction is quantization at zero rate. \(c_0=1\) is derived, not posited; H2O and KIVI become corner solutions to one program. confirmed
  6. The value term is decorative. The \(\|v_i-o\|\) factor leaves the ladder unchanged on all six models, so allocation needs only attention weights and never touches the value cache. We built this as an ablation against our own design and it simplified the method. measured
  7. Unbiased quantizers make bad indices. QJL's variance penalty pushes its 1-bit tier above the cost of eviction and its 2-bit tier off the convex envelope, costing about two rungs of ladder at every \(\tau\) — an independent explanation for the deployed backend deleting it. confirmed
  8. Analytic models of attention mislead here. Gaussian logits lock \(\varphi\) to \(\tau\) and produce a qualitatively wrong regime map. Real logits are heavy-tailed and the statistics decouple. predicted wrong, then explained

A framework that only reorganizes known results gets scored as a survey. This one predicted a number to 3.4% on six production models, predicted where it would stop working on both sides, and was wrong about the variable that indexes the boundary — which is how we found the right one.

What is not yet settled

stated plainly, because reviewers will find these

  • Context length is confounded with model identity. The three 128k runs are the three lowest band fractions. If the band narrows with context, the method works best where the KV cache is least painful. A single-model sweep from 8k to 128k settles it in a day and runs next.
  • The two largest models are the two worst. The failures have opposite mechanisms, which argues against a simple scale effect, but with six models it is not resolved.
  • 2-bit scores do not rank within the head region. Spearman \(\rho\) is 0.47–0.56 against a 0.9 threshold, so the coarse pass can separate head from tail but not order the head. The base layer moves to 3 bits.
  • The linearized cost overstates the gain by up to 4.9×. It remains the correct allocation rule and is not a usable predictor of the benefit. Every number here is measured.
  • The eviction corner is an oracle ranking by true sensitivity — stronger than any deployable evictor. Replacing it with a lagged-attention evictor can only widen the band.
  • Output error is not end-task accuracy. 2× lower output error has to survive translation to RULER and AIME at matched bandwidth before the systems claim means anything.

Roughly six weeks to a submission-grade result: context sweep and practical evictor (3 days), 3-bit base layer and nested-coding overhead (10 days), GQA union cost (1 week), then the kernel and the iso-budget ablation on hardware (4 weeks) — with a kill gate at every stage.
