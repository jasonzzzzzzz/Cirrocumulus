ICLR 2027 · project pitch · v6 · 25 model×context points, SYMMETRIC comparison, 4k–262k · design fixed · end-task in progress

KV cache compression has a
phase diagram, and context length moves you across it.

Quantize every token evenly, or throw most of them away — the field has argued between these for three years. They are corner solutions to one rate-allocation problem, each optimal in its own regime. We derive the boundary variable, show that a single model crosses the phases as context grows, and measure what allocation is worth when both sides of the comparison are restricted to information a deployed system actually has.

Abstract

what the paper says

Serving long-context LLMs is bounded by the KV cache, and every method that shrinks it — quantization or token eviction — minimizes key reconstruction error. We show this is the wrong distortion measure. Posed against attention-output error, KV compression becomes a rate-allocation problem whose solution is reverse water-filling: the optimal budget for a token is linear in the log of its attention weight, and eviction emerges as the zero-rate case of the same equation, with cost constant \(c_0=1\) derived rather than posited. Comparing \(\tau^2c_b\) against that 1 yields both a phase diagram and its boundary variable: the fraction of heads whose low tiers are extinct. We measure it on 25 model×context configurations, six architectures, 4k–262k, in a symmetric comparison: the allocator and the eviction baseline both score on lagged attention, so neither is handed information a deployed system lacks. The dead-tier fraction orders the outcome at Spearman \(\rho=-0.978\) pooled, \(-0.915\) with model identity partialled out and \(-1.00\) within every model, and the linearized cost model forward-predicts the measured output-error gain to 0.80–1.42×. Context length is itself a phase variable — llama3.1-8B loses 34 band points from 8k to 128k with nothing else changed — and \(\tau\) is convex in \(\log L\), with a small extra penalty for models run at 100% of their RoPE window. Three design questions are now measured rather than assumed: per-head rates are realizable under GQA (1.14× at n_rep 4, 1.32× at 8, against a 3× kill line — and the same constraint costs eviction more), a cascade score off the 4-bit base tier recovers ~89% of what lagged information costs the allocator, and the router and the allocator run on different clocks: the route survives 4,096 generated tokens while a frozen allocation costs 2.5–4.3×. What remains open is end-task accuracy, now in progress.

The problem

why the field is stuck

At 128k+ context the KV cache dominates memory and decode bandwidth. Two families attack it, they disagree, and both are right — in different places, which nobody had established:

FamilyAllocatesHow it fails
Eviction
H2O, SnapKV		{0, 16} bitsDrops tokens a future query will need. Missing one critical key produces sharp error spikes in long-output reasoning.
Quantization
KIVI, QJL, TurboQuant		{b, b} bitsAggressive variants lose up to 20 points on AIME25 and ~30% relative on 256k retrieval, and pay 10–68% latency for dequantization. FP8 beats all of them on the Pareto frontier.

Both fix the allocation a priori, then optimize a proxy objective — how faithfully a key is reproduced — that attention never evaluates.

Attention does not consume keys. It consumes \(o=\sum_i a_i v_i\). A key carrying 0.4 of the mass and a key carrying \(10^{-6}\) are not equally worth storing, and no reconstruction metric can tell them apart.

Swap the distortion measure and the answer is classical. With \(\operatorname{Var}(\delta_i)=\tau^2c_b\) and \(\partial o/\partial s_i = a_i(v_i-o)\), minimizing \(\mathbb{E}\|\Delta o\|^2\) under \(\sum_i b_i\le B\) is reverse water-filling:

$$b_i^\star=\log_2 a_i + c\,,\qquad b_i^\star = 0 \ \text{ below the water level.}$$

Dropping a set and renormalizing costs exactly \(\sum_{i\in E}a_i^2\|o_R-v_i\|^2\), so eviction's cost constant is \(c_0=1\), derived. For the first time eviction and quantization are the same quantity on the same axis — and comparing \(\tau^2c_b\) against that 1 is what produces the phase diagram.

The interior does not lose uniformly when it is denied the current step — it loses exactly where it was winning. Out-of-band heads pay 1.00–1.04×, in-band heads 1.13–1.64×, and \(\rho(\text{lag cost},\text{gain})=+0.50\ldots+0.97\) in 16/16 cells. A head is in band because its sensitivity is concentrated somewhere a flat allocation cannot exploit, and that concentration is what moves between steps. The interior's advantage and its need for current-query information are the same property — which is why the design re-budgets during decode and scores the re-budget off the base tier.

The Qwen3 family is pinned in the eviction corner at every context length — 60–73% dead tiers at 8k as readily as at 128k — because its attention is sink-dominated (median top-1 weight ≈ 0.5). That is an architectural phase, not a context effect, and the same axis separates it from the context-driven kind.

two predictions we got wrong, and the second one we caught ourselves v5 indexed the regime by \(\tau\). Wrong: \(\tau\) alone reaches only \(\rho=-0.74\), because two models can share a \(\tau\) and differ fourfold in outcome. v6 replaced it with effective support \(\varphi=n_{95}/L\). Also wrong, and worse — on valid data \(\varphi\) correlates with the outcome at \(+0.26\), the wrong sign, because \(\varphi\) divides by \(L\) and so conflates the architecture with the context length it was measured at. The variable that works was in the theory the whole time: push \(\tau\) through the tier-extinction test \(\tau^2c_b\) vs \(c_0=1\) and the result is \(L\)-free, derived, and predicts 20 points at \(-0.96\). We report this because the correction is the finding: an index chosen for fit gets overturned by the next campaign; one derived from the cost comparison does not.

Context length is a phase variable

the result that reframes the paper

Every prior version of this work reported one verdict per model. That is ill-posed. Sweeping llama3.1-8B across six points with nothing else changed, in the symmetric comparison:

The causal chain is mechanistic and every link is measured: \(\tau\) rises with \(L\) in all six models, which pushes \(\tau^2c_b\) past \(c_0=1\) for progressively higher rungs, so the dead-tier fraction climbs, the ladder loses rungs from the bottom, and the optimum degenerates toward the two-tier solution that is eviction.

A per-model verdict is not a well-posed object. The router must condition on (architecture, context) — and the phase diagram is what makes that a lookup rather than a re-measurement.

a prediction we made, pre-registered, and then had to reverse Is the long-context collapse real, or an artifact of running models at their trained limit? Every model that reaches 128k drops sharply there, and the two models that dropped most were at 100% of their RoPE window while the one with 2× headroom barely moved. We wrote the test before the cells ran: read \(\tau\) against a quadratic-in-\(\log_2 L\) null fitted to each model's pre-cap points, thresholds fixed in advance, with cells at 196k and 262k on the one model with headroom.

The answer is mostly ABSOLUTE-L, and it reverses our earlier reading. \(\tau\) is convex in \(\log L\), so the straight-line extrapolations that produced the original "cap effect" manufactured one; the dead-tier rates we had cited are a threshold artifact. What survives is a real but modest +0.21 \(\tau\) excess at exactly rope_frac = 1.00, none at 0.75. The decay with context is real, steep and smooth — and the "37-point collapse in the final octave" we headlined is withdrawn as a mechanism claim.
what a band number means — our own error budget A verdict at a 2× threshold on a per-head median is fragile, so we measured its error budget instead of asserting it. Prompts move the band by 0.4–3.0 points (six prompts suffice; an earlier two-prompt screen predicted 9.6 and was wrong). The corner set moves it by 5–9 points and changes verdicts in three of four cells. So "the band is X%" is not a measurement until the corner set is named — we report the five-corner minimum as the defensible competitor, under which llama3.1-8B @128k becomes the one borderline cell.

The router is the product, and the phase diagram is what makes it cheap. A model is a mixture of phases, and which phase a head is in depends on the deployment context length as much as on the architecture. The compressor's job is to read each head's phase from a one-pass offline calibration — the same forward-pass hook that produced this data — and apply the locally optimal scheme. Because the boundary variable is derived and \(L\)-free, that calibration extrapolates across context lengths instead of needing a fresh sweep per deployment.

Two properties make this deployable rather than merely true. The value term \(\|v_i-o\|\) is measured to be decorative (ladder shifts by at most 0.035 bits when it is dropped), so allocation reads attention weights and never touches the value cache. And the floor is 1.0×: outside the band the router hands the head to the corner that owns it, so the method declines rather than loses.

Novel claims

each falsifiable; five now tested on production models

  1. The objective is wrong. KV compression should minimize attention-output distortion, not key reconstruction distortion. Everything follows from this substitution. theory
  2. Eviction is quantization at zero rate. \(c_0=1\) is derived, not posited; H2O and KIVI become corner solutions to one program, on one axis, for the first time. confirmed
  3. KV compression has three phases, and the boundary variable is derived from the same cost comparison — the fraction of heads with an extinct 2-bit tier, \(\tau^2c_2>c_0\). It contains no \(L\) and orders the band at \(\rho=-0.978\) over 25 configurations spanning six architectures and 4k–262k of context, \(-0.915\) with model identity partialled out and \(-1.00\) within every model. Restated here: it is an ordering claim plus an architecture-specific threshold — nine cells run to pin the boundary turned its GO bracket from a gap into an overlap, with per-model crossings 5.4 points apart, so a universal cut would have been wrong in both directions. measured, and restated
  4. Context length is a phase variable, acting through \(\tau\) — and \(\tau\) is convex in \(\log L\). Rising \(\tau\) extinguishes rungs from the bottom; llama3.1-8B loses 34 band points from 8k to 128k with nothing else changed, and per-model verdicts are ill-posed. Tested against a pre-registered curved null: the extra penalty for running a model at 100% of its RoPE window is real but small (+0.21 in \(\tau\)), and the "accelerating collapse" we previously headlined was linear extrapolation plus a threshold artifact. measured, and partly reversed
  5. Optimal precision is logarithmic in attention mass, \(b_i^\star=\log_2 a_i+c\), and the cost model it comes from forward-predicts the measured output-error gain to 0.80–1.42× across all 27 configurations. (The ladder width \(\tau/\ln2\) is an algebraic identity, not evidence — appendix consistency check only.) measured
  6. Tail dependence is architectural, and no fixed budget serves a model set. We proposed that eviction’s fixed-fraction budget was mis-specified and that an absolute-support budget would restore the band. We built the test and it is false: \(K^\*\), the smallest keep-count landing within 10% of the full-budget corner, is 100% of the budget at every model and every context — there is no slack to reclaim. What the diagnostic returned instead is better: \(K^\*/n_{95}\) spans 37× (5.8 for mistral-7b to 214.7 for llama3.3-70B) and is not ordered by support — the 70B has the smallest \(n_{95}\) in the study and the deepest tail. Per-head keep-counts must come from measured support, not a constant. proposed, tested, refuted — and replaced
  7. Per-head rates are realizable under GQA, and the constraint favours us. Heads sharing a KV head cannot be given independent rates, which could have made "per-head allocation" fictional on five of our six models. Measured: 1.000 / 1.136 / 1.316× at n_rep 1 / 4 / 8 against a 3× kill line, monotone in group size, with an exact n_rep = 1 control — and the same constraint costs eviction 4.3–6.5 band points more than it costs the allocator. measured
  8. The router and the allocator run on different clocks. Over 4,096 generated tokens the per-head route is stable (p90 regret 1.00, six cells, two architectures, sampled and greedy) while a frozen allocation costs 2.5–4.3×. So: one offline calibration pass, plus a decode-time re-budgeter whose score comes off the 4-bit base tier and recovers ~89% of the penalty without reading full-precision keys. measured
  9. The value term is decorative. The \(\|v_i-o\|\) factor moves the ladder by at most 0.035 bits, so allocation needs only attention weights and never touches the value cache. We built this as an ablation against our own design and it simplified the method. measured
  10. Unbiased quantizers make bad indices. QJL's variance penalty pushes its 1-bit tier above the cost of eviction and its 2-bit tier off the convex envelope — an independent explanation for the deployed backend deleting it. confirmed
  11. Scale does not hurt; context does. At matched context the 70B is the best model — 87.4% at 8k and 70.0% at 32k in the symmetric comparison (83.6% / 60.9% all-oracle). The opposite trend we reported in v6 was context length in disguise. reversed by matched-ctx data
  12. Attention measurement is a methodological minefield, and we have the receipts. Three separate defects each silently produced a plausible, wrong regime map: a synthetic haystack, a top-left-aligned causal mask under chunked prefill, and a validity gate keyed on a bulk moment that a single needle cannot move. Each is documented with the bisect that found it. found, fixed, recorded

A framework that only reorganizes known results gets scored as a survey. This one forward-predicts an exactly-recomputed error ratio to within −20%/+42% across 27 model×context configurations, derives its own boundary variable rather than fitting one, and has now discarded two candidate indices — including one we published in the previous revision — because the derived quantity beat them both.

What is not yet settled

stated plainly, because reviewers will find these

  • Output error is not end-task accuracy — and the first end-task pilot moved the experiment, not the method. This is the largest gap between the paper and a deployment argument, and it is now being closed. The pilot (llama3.1-8B @32k, RULER-style retrieval and multi-hop, the model generating from a genuinely compressed cache) says: full precision answers 0.99–1.00, uniform quantization fails at 1 bit and is perfect from 2 — and SnapKV scores 1.00 at every budget. The reason is a protocol finding, not a result about allocation: when the question sits inside the window the evictor scores with, eviction is an oracle on retrieval benchmarks. The honest test compresses the context before the question exists — the prefix-caching and multi-turn regime — and that is the run now in flight, with a sub-1-bit budget where eviction and allocation are the only arms that exist.
  • The band narrows with context, and five of sixteen cells are STOP. In the symmetric comparison the 128k row reads 21.3% / 20.2% / 7.5%. Allocation helps most below ~64k, where the KV cache hurts least — and under the strongest corner set we can build (a minimum over five evictors) llama3.1-8B @128k becomes borderline. The science survives this; the deployment reach narrows.
  • The boundary is pinned per architecture, not universally. Nine cells run specifically to close the GO bracket turned it from a gap into an overlap: at the same dead-2 fraction, two models differ by 8 band points. dead-2 orders the band almost perfectly, but its critical value has to be fitted per architecture — which the calibration pass can do, and which a "45%" constant could not.
  • 2-bit scores do not rank within the head region. Spearman \(\rho\) is 0.52–0.77 against a 0.9 threshold, so the coarse pass separates head from tail but does not order it. The base layer is 3–4 bits — and the cascade result independently says 4.
  • Nesting overhead is unmeasured. Equitz–Cover is asymptotic; we use per-coordinate Lloyd-Max at \(d{=}128\). A 20% rate penalty would remove the storage architecture's foundation.
  • Within-generation \(\tau\) drift is underpowered. Whether \(\tau\) moves during a generation because of content or because the cache grows cannot be told apart at 3–6 prompts; an earlier claim that it matched the cache-growth prediction is withdrawn.

What is left, in order: finish the end-task run that is in flight (days), then the four published eviction baselines already built against it; the \(K^\*\)-derived per-head budget; nested-coding overhead; and last the kernel and the iso-budget ablation against dense FP8 — with a kill gate at each stage.
